The credit score system does not exist in France, so it feels mysterious when you arrive. And it matters, because landlords and lenders check it.

Here is how I see it: the credit system is partly a way for banks to make money off people who spend money they do not have and then pay it back late with fees and interest. It is also useful for customers, because it lets you pay for things upfront and pay later.

I was lucky to get a solid financial education from my parents: save, understand the value of money, and never spend money you do not have. With that mindset, building a credit score was pretty easy.

## What I did

1. **Opened an account at a big national bank** with branches everywhere. You may need an in person appointment to open your first account, so a bank with physical branches makes life easier.
2. **Gave an address.** You need one to open the account. I did not have a permanent address yet, so I used my work address. You can put an address and change it later.
3. **Got a credit card** through the bank.
4. **Set up automatic payments** for the full balance every month, so I never paid interest or late fees.
5. **Used it for normal spending** and let the history build.

Your French bank history does not transfer. American banks and landlords do not care about it at all. You start at zero, so start as soon as you land.

## Is a credit card worth it?

Yes, if you manage it properly. I now have two cards (an Apple Card and a Chase card), and on the one I use consistently I get about $10 to $15 back per month in cash back. That is free money as long as you pay in full, on time, every month.

## The warning

It is really easy to lose track. A card makes spending feel painless. Keep an eye on your expenses and treat the card like a debit card: only spend what is already in your account.
