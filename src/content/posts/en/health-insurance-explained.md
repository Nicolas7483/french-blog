Health is the biggest difference of all between France and the US.

In France, healthcare is mostly public. In the US, hospitals and healthcare are largely private businesses, and like any business in the US, they need to make a profit. One thing that struck me early: you see advertising for hospitals everywhere, which you basically never see in France.

## How it usually works when you have a job

* Your employer offers the health insurance plans they work with.
* Part of your pay goes to that insurance, typically a couple hundred dollars per paycheck or per month depending on the plan.
* You pick a tier. Usually there is a low, mid and high tier plan.
* You can add dental and vision on top.

## How to pick

Do not just pick the cheapest plan. Pick the one that fits you. Know yourself and what you are likely to need: a condition you already have, regular prescriptions, how often you see a doctor. The right plan depends on that.

These plans can be intimidating and honestly feel designed to be confusing. Even HR people sometimes have a hard time explaining them. Today you can use AI tools to go through the plan documents and explain the differences in plain language. Do it. Sit down and actually decode your options.

As far as I understand, you are not strictly limited to the plan your company offers; there are other ways to buy insurance. But do your research, because the rules and costs vary.

## Save for it

Nothing is going to be fully paid for by the government like in France. Keep savings aside for when you actually need care, because even with insurance you will pay part of the bill.

## What it taught me

I am young, so I do not expect to need much right now. But this system taught me that health is not a given. It is something you maintain every day. It pushed me to train more, eat better and adapt my lifestyle. And if I do need care, I know roughly what it is going to cost me.
