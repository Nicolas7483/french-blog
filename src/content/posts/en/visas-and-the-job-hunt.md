Of everything on this site, the visa is the only part that is truly hard. To work in the US, you usually need a company willing to sponsor your visa. That is the real bottleneck of the job hunt.

## Know which visa fits you

There are many visa types depending on who you are: athletes, artists and creatives, entrepreneurs and investors, trainees, skilled workers, and more. Each has its own rules, length and restrictions. Before you start looking, learn which ones fit your profile and your industry, because that shapes which companies can realistically hire you.

A few that come up often for young French people:

* **VIE (Volontariat International en Entreprise):** a French program that lets you work abroad for a French company. It is a common door into the US for young French graduates. Look it up on the [Business France site](https://mon-vie-via.businessfrance.fr/).
* **J1:** an exchange visitor visa that covers interns and trainees, among other categories, for a limited period (the trainee category can run up to 18 months).
* **E2:** a treaty visa. Because France has a treaty with the US, employees of companies that are majority French owned can qualify in certain roles. It is tied to the company, has to be renewed, and comes with paperwork each time.

This is not legal advice. Visa rules change and every case is different. Check the official sources ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/), [uscis.gov](https://www.uscis.gov/) and the [US Embassy in France](https://fr.usembassy.gov/)) and talk to an immigration attorney before you make decisions.

## How I found my job: let them come to you

Instead of only sending applications, I posted my resume on LinkedIn with a short description of what I could do and what I was looking for. A French company with a US branch reached out to me, and they were willing to sponsor the visa.

That reverse approach worked better for me than cold applying. French companies with US offices are a natural fit, because they already understand French profiles and French programs.

## About paid job hunting programs

Before that, I paid around $100 for a job hunting program built by someone who had made it to the US himself. The method was systematic: list target companies in a spreadsheet, contact them directly (including on WhatsApp), and pitch yourself clearly, including what visa you need.

I applied it fully and seriously. It did not land me a job. To his credit, the program had a refund policy and he refunded me when I showed him I had done the work.

My takeaway: no method guarantees a job, because the sponsorship question decides most of it. Be careful before paying for a program, and check whether it offers a refund.
