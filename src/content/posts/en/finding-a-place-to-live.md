Finding a place when you first arrive is hard. You have no US credit history, no rental history and often no one on the ground.

## When you first land: Facebook Marketplace

Early on, you often do not have much choice other than Facebook Marketplace. Think of it as the American Le Bon Coin for rentals and rooms.

There are a lot of scams. Some rules:

* Learn the red flags (price too good to be true, pressure to pay before you see anything, excuses for why you cannot visit).
* Call the person. Do not do everything over messages.
* If you know anyone locally, have them visit the place for you.
* At the very least, ask for a FaceTime tour of the actual place.

There are no real guarantees on Marketplace. It is a jungle.

## Apartment complexes: read the fees

Once you move into a proper apartment complex, it does not get simpler. There are a lot of charges on top of the base rent. If the base rent is $1,500, plan for around $500 more per month once everything is added. Leases have many conditions nobody reads. Use AI to go through the lease and list every fee and condition before you sign.

## You can negotiate

People do not realize how much room there often is. Apartment complexes have flexible pricing and they want tenants. It will not work every time, but the price is not always fixed.

What worked for us: we got a better rent by signing a longer lease. Management values a stable tenant who pays on time, so lease length is real leverage.

## Credit history comes first

Any real apartment complex or residential property will check your credit history. Your French banking history does not count at all. You need a US bank account and a US credit history.

So the order is:

1. Open a US bank account as soon as you arrive (see [the credit score article](../building-a-credit-score-from-zero/)).
2. Get a credit card and use it responsibly.
3. After a couple of months, you have enough history to pass most rental checks.

Property managers also use different credit checking systems, so every application can play out differently.

## Have a plan B

Plan for this to take time. Keep several options open and do not tie yourself to a single property. If an approval drags on, you need somewhere to stay in the meantime.
