Tipping has annoyed me for a long time.

In France, when you pay at a restaurant, you expect that check to cover everything: the waiter, the cook, the staff. In the US, many states (Florida included) allow restaurants to pay servers a lower minimum wage than other workers, because tips are expected to make up the difference. In effect, the restaurant owner shifts part of the responsibility for a stable income onto the customer. I think that is unfair. When you pay for your meal, the check should pay for everything.

## Tip screens are everywhere

Almost every place now has a touch screen at checkout (Toast and similar systems) that asks for a tip on every transaction. Good to know:

* The business chooses the default options. They can set them to 10, 20, 30, even 40 percent.
* You can always choose no tip. It is an option on the screen.

The screen puts the pressure on you. Saying no feels awkward, and it is psychologically harder to say no than yes, so a lot of people tip out of politeness. You should not feel obligated to tip on a $7 coffee at a counter. I now see these prompts at coffee chains and even in some grocery stores.

## When you should tip

At a sit down restaurant with table service, not tipping is culturally wrong, and my partner and I have talked about this a lot. The system is unfair, but if you skip the tip, the person you punish is the waiter, not the owner. Without tips, servers do not earn a decent wage.

## My approach

* If the bill is around $20, I tip about 15 percent.
* Above that, I tip a fixed amount of a few dollars rather than a percentage. A $50 meal for one person is the same amount of work for the server as a $20 meal, so I do not think the tip should scale with the price.

**Heads up:** most Americans tip a percentage, usually 15 to 20 percent at a sit down restaurant, and servers expect that. A fixed amount on a bigger bill will land below what they are used to. Decide with that in mind.

The goal is balance: do not overthink it, do not punish the server, but do not tip so much on everything that you stop wanting to go out. I rarely eat at sit down restaurants. Most of the time I eat at counter service places or get takeout, where there is no expectation to tip.

Fun fact: tip prompts are starting to show up in France too, including in a lot of Paris restaurants.
